## 1. There is no visible high-frequency or endpoint escape

For the cutoff-free c=100 ground vectors:

| N | periodic log-energy | outer 25% frequency mass |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 8 | 1.29013221325 | 9.61e-6 |
| 12 | 1.33094950080 | 1.18e-9 |
| 16 | 1.35801177593 | 4.42e-13 |
| 24 | 1.39183288617 | 1.27e-21 |
| 32 | 1.41202516144 | 1.46e-29 |
| 48 | 1.43437789400 | 4.19e-43 |

The endpoint-strip mass falls even faster. For the 5% endpoint strips:

```text
N=24: 3.11e-41
N=32: 9.14e-50
N=48: 2.63e-64
```

The source value at the endpoint tends numerically to zero extremely rapidly, while the interior value at `s=1/2` grows mildly:

```text
T_N(0):   1.48e-15 (N=8) -> 1.57e-57 (N=48)
T_N(1/2): 2.57       (N=8) -> 3.24       (N=48)
```

Therefore the false finite-T picture of a high-frequency/boundary escape disappears once the archimedean term is assembled cutoff-free.

## 2. The vectors are strongly nested, but not trivially frozen

After sign alignment and zero-padding the smaller vector into the larger band:

| transition | overlap | L2 distance | mass in newly available modes |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 8 -> 12 | 0.994620 | 0.10373 | 1.18e-9 |
| 12 -> 16 | 0.997933 | 0.06430 | 5.14e-15 |
| 16 -> 24 | 0.997121 | 0.07588 | 7.22e-19 |
| 24 -> 32 | 0.999065 | 0.04325 | 4.97e-30 |
| 32 -> 48 | 0.998931 | 0.04623 | 2.92e-36 |

The striking feature is the mismatch of scales:

> the newly added modes carry essentially no norm, yet allowing them changes the optimized low-frequency coefficient vector at the percent-to-per-mille level and drives the smallest eigenvalue down by many decimal orders.

This is not ordinary mass escape. It is a **global cancellation / Schur-complement renormalization** phenomenon.

## 3. Relation to Bombieri's zero-mode channel

Bombieri observed that finite restricted eigenvalues may tend to zero while their eigenvectors converge toward a constrained linear-dependence relation generated by zeta zeros.

The c=100 data are qualitatively much closer to that mechanism than to frequency escape:

- finite minima are rigorously positive but plunge toward zero;
- log-energy remains modest;
- endpoint and outer-band masses vanish rapidly;
- nested vectors have overlaps near one;
- the ground state remains isolated relative to its already tiny eigenvalue.

However, the present data do **not** prove convergence to a nonzero infinite-dimensional zero mode. In particular, the overlaps are not monotone in the irregular band sequence, and the continuum minimum at this support could still be an astronomically small positive number rather than zero.

## 4. Why the smallest eigenvalue is the wrong primary coordinate

At c=100 we rigorously see

```text
lambda_8  ~ 4.34e-32
lambda_16 ~ 4.86e-53
lambda_32 ~ 1.58e-87
lambda_48 ~ 2.05e-116
```

The eigenvector changes are mild while the eigenvalue changes are enormous. This suggests that the tiny eigenvalue is a derived cancellation scale, not a stable quantity for analysis.

The natural next coordinate is the scalar **Schur pivot** added by each new even Fourier mode. That isolates exactly how a tiny new degree of freedom renormalizes the old block.

## Current conclusion

The best current empirical description is:

```text
cutoff-free ground states are increasingly interior and spectrally concentrated,
while tiny newly admitted coefficients trigger global cancellation in the old modes.
```

This motivates the Schur-complement / Loewner-matrix program in note 011.
