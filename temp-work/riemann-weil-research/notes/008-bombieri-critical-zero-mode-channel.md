# 008 — Bombieri critical zero-mode channel

## Why this note exists

The generic compactness route is too weak to prove global Weil positivity. The next useful reduction is to isolate exactly what can happen at the first support value where the restricted Weil minimum reaches zero.

Primary source: Enrico Bombieri, *Remarks on Weil's quadratic functional in the theory of prime numbers, I*, Rendiconti Lincei Mat. Appl. 11 (2000), 183–233.

Public copy used for this reading:

- https://www.bdim.eu/item?id=RLIN_2000_9_11_3_183_0
- https://paperzz.com/doc/7088948/remarks-on-weil-s-quadratic-functional-in-the-theory-of-p...

## 1. The support minimum already has the right global structure

Bombieri proves that the even and odd restricted minima `mu_+(M)` and `mu_-(M)` are continuous decreasing functions of the multiplicative support radius `M`.

Consequently, if global Weil positivity fails, the support-restricted problem admits a first critical support radius in the natural sense:

```text
positive region -> first zero level -> negative region
```

This validates the `first crossing` viewpoint, but it does **not** solve the problem: the zero level can be reached through a limiting eigenvector/linear-dependence mechanism.

## 2. Bombieri's first eigenvalue equation

For an extremal in his first eigenvalue formulation, Bombieri obtains on `(M^-1, M)`

```text
lambda D f = L[f]
```

and, after the explicit formula,

```text
-lambda (x^2 f'(x))' = sum_rho fhat(rho) x^{-rho},
```

with boundary conditions

```text
f(M) = f(M^-1) = 0.
```

He also proves strong interior regularity for an eigenfunction and quadratic decay of its Mellin transform in vertical strips. Thus a genuine limiting zero/negative mode is substantially more structured than an arbitrary low-energy `L2` function.

## 3. The dangerous limiting channel is not ordinary boundary concentration

In Sections 10–11 Bombieri studies finite eigenvalue approximations. A negative finite eigenvalue may tend to zero. In that case the limiting object can collapse into a linear-dependence relation among the exponential functions generated by zeta zeros.

In logarithmic coordinates this is the exponential system

```text
F(u) = sum_gamma z_gamma exp(-i gamma u)
```

with coefficients constrained by the eigenproblem. Bombieri explicitly notes that the limiting zero-eigenvalue possibility is tied to linear dependence, and that such relations cannot be ruled out merely by generic compactness. He even points out that shrinking zero gaps make linear relations over intervals plausible in sufficiently broad settings.

Therefore the strongest version of our earlier idea is false:

> `bounded low energy + compact support` alone cannot force a nonzero critical mode that yields a contradiction.

The object to exclude is narrower:

> a **zeta-zero-generated critical mode satisfying the exact Weil/Bombieri coefficient equations**.

## 4. Updated research target

Assume a first critical support exists. Let `f_c` be a normalized minimizer with zero restricted Weil value (or, in a finite-to-infinite limit, let the candidate coefficient sequence be the corresponding zero-mode limit).

We need one of the following:

1. **Zero-mode rigidity:** prove that the exact coefficient/eigenvalue equations plus zeta-zero structure force `f_c = 0`, contradicting normalization.
2. **Quantitative no-collapse:** prove that a finite sequence of near-zero eigenvectors cannot converge to one of Bombieri's linear-dependence relations while retaining the normalization/mass constraints coming from the Weil problem.
3. **Certified counterexample:** find a convergent, energy-controlled finite sequence whose cutoff-free Weil value stays strictly negative by an interval certificate. That would instead point toward a disproof candidate.

## 5. Why the current CvS/Groskin diagnostics are relevant

The CvS/Groskin coefficient vector reconstructs an explicit compact-support source `T_v`, and the finite matrix value is linked exactly to a Guinand–Weil test function. Therefore we can attach to every finite ground vector:

- cutoff-free or certified eigenvalue information;
- outer-frequency mass;
- logarithmic source energy;
- endpoint-strip mass;
- vector overlap between successive `(N,T)` approximations.

The last item is now especially important. Bombieri reports that when a negative eigenvalue tends to zero, the associated eigenvector may converge rapidly. We should distinguish two phenomena:

```text
A. truncation artifact:
   eigenvalue sign changes under archimedean-tail correction / T ladder,
   vector changes strongly or moves to high frequencies.

B. genuine zero-mode candidate:
   cutoff-free value approaches 0,
   source energy remains controlled,
   successive eigenvectors have overlap -> 1,
   reconstructed source converges strongly,
   and the Bombieri/CvS zero-source equations remain satisfied.
```

## 6. Immediate experiment upgrade

For a fixed `c` and increasing `N`, record not only the ground eigenvalue and source energy but also:

```text
abs(<v_N, pad(v_{N-small})>)
```

and the `L2` distance of the reconstructed sources after phase/sign alignment.

Do the same under the finite-`T` ladder after applying the rigorous Groskin tail budget.

A stable eigenvector with an uncertified tiny finite-`T` negative eigenvalue is not evidence against RH. A stable eigenvector whose **cutoff-free interval-certified eigenvalue is negative** would be qualitatively different.

## Current conclusion

The critical-window idea survives, but the target is now precise:

```text
rule out (or certify) the Bombieri zero-mode / linear-dependence channel,
not generic endpoint concentration.
```
