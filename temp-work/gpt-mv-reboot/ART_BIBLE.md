# Art bible — GPT MV reboot

## 1. Visual thesis

The film should feel like **an editorial illustration studio coming alive around a conversation**.

Primary references in spirit are printmaking, magazine illustration, architectural drafting, physical collage, tracing paper, book design, stop-motion paper craft, and contemporary character animation — not science-fiction UI.

The visual pleasure comes from material transformation:
- a sentence becomes a folded paper street;
- a correction line becomes a bridge support;
- a dragged image becomes a window in the world;
- a diagram becomes a machine with real weight;
- erased graphite remains faintly visible and later becomes useful structure.

The key phrase is **tactile intelligence**.

## 2. GPT character sheet

### Overall read

A recognisable protagonist at silhouette size, modern and approachable. Human enough for acting; stylised enough to live inside graphic layouts.

### Proportions

- young adult, around 7.5 heads tall;
- medium/slim build, grounded rather than fashion-model elongated;
- hands slightly oversized for readable acting;
- shoulders relaxed;
- face designed for clear eyebrow/eye acting at medium shot.

### Hair

- short dark bob ending around jaw/upper neck;
- one asymmetric longer lock or folded cobalt fabric strip on the right side;
- no glowing strands;
- hair mass reads as one strong dark shape at thumbnail size.

### Clothes

Base outfit:
- warm off-white overshirt/work jacket;
- graphite inner top;
- dark straight trousers or simple skirt, chosen after silhouette comparison;
- cobalt seam/tape accent limited to one or two lines;
- small folded-ribbon brooch near collarbone;
- plain dark shoes.

The outfit may collect physical traces during the film: pencil dust, tape tab, tiny paper clipping. It does not transform into armor.

### Face and expressions

Face is always present unless intentionally occluded by composition.

Expression vocabulary:
- **listen**: neutral mouth, eye focus toward user mark, slight forward head;
- **parse**: eyes moving between fragments, one brow raised;
- **play**: small asymmetrical smile, quick glance toward user action;
- **confidence**: level eyes, economical motion, shoulders open;
- **misread**: stillness first, pupils shift to failed object, mouth stops mid-thought;
- **realize**: brow softens, short inhale, hand changes task;
- **correct**: concentrated, not embarrassed caricature;
- **shared success**: relief more than triumph;
- **quiet end**: relaxed attentive face, not melancholy abandonment.

Avoid anime stock expressions, glowing eyes, deadpan android acting, and permanent smile.

## 3. The user

The user is a co-author, not a disembodied command source.

Visible vocabulary:
- sunflower-yellow insertion point;
- cursor arrow / text caret;
- selection highlight;
- hand-drawn underline;
- sticky note;
- clipped reference image;
- moved ruler, page, or physical object;
- empty chair / coffee ring / notebook edge on the opposite side of desk;
- occasional short sans-serif text fragment when necessary.

Never show a face or full human body. Never turn the user into a glowing orb.

## 4. Motif system

### Folded ribbon

GPT's recurring mark is a small folded strip making a loose four-turn loop. It is deliberately **not** the OpenAI/ChatGPT logo.

Uses:
- collar brooch;
- page corner mark;
- a fold pattern in WORLD scenes;
- registration target in TRACE;
- final two-author page contains one cobalt fold and one sunflower mark.

The motif may physically unfold into longer strips. It does not rotate forever as a loading spinner.

### Margin / crop marks

Corners, crop lines, page numbers, and editorial margins provide rhythm and continuity. They are functional composition tools, not HUD decoration.

### Transparent proof sheet

Tracing-paper layers are the core visual metaphor for alternatives and revision. Earlier versions remain faintly visible and can become part of later solutions.

## 5. Palette

Use flat local colour with physical light/shadow. Avoid neon bloom.

Suggested starting values; final values must be tuned on actual style frames:

- paper warm: `#F3EFE4`
- paper shadow: `#D8D1C2`
- graphite: `#232426`
- graphite soft: `#5D6064`
- cobalt/GPT: `#3157D5`
- cobalt light: `#7891E8`
- sunflower/user: `#E8B928`
- sunflower light: `#F4D96F`
- vermilion/correction: `#D94B3D`
- completion green: `#5E9A79` — rare, late, never dominant

### Palette tests

Before production, render a chart containing:
- GPT face and clothes;
- user cursor/selection;
- correction marks;
- paper under warm and cool lighting;
- all colours at thumbnail scale and in grayscale.

Cobalt and sunflower must remain separable under mild desaturation. Vermilion must never be confused with sunflower.

## 6. Materials and texture

### Paper

- visible but subtle fibre/noise;
- real edge thickness on foreground sheets;
- slight warping or curl when lifted;
- shadows are soft and directional, not ambient-glow halos.

### Glass

Used sparingly for table surface, frame, or pinned overlay. Reflections should describe space and camera angle.

### Pencil / grease pencil

- graphite construction marks use varied pressure;
- correction vermilion looks printed/grease-pencil, not laser UI red;
- erasure leaves residue.

### Ink / printed colour

Important WORLD illustrations can use misregistration by 1–3 px as a tactile print effect, but only when it supports the scene. Persistent glitch is forbidden.

## 7. Environments

### Shared studio

Default DESK location:
- large pale worktable occupying lower third to half of frame;
- tall diffuse daylight from left or rear-left;
- shelves/walls mostly quiet so paper activity reads;
- opposite user side contains hints of occupancy, not a person;
- physical depth supports over-the-shoulder, profile, top-down, and wide shots.

The studio may gradually accumulate the conversation's artifacts. Continuity matters.

### WORLD spaces

Each WORLD shot must choose a concrete subject. Candidate visual families:
- paper city unfolding from a map;
- typographic rain becoming real rain on a greenhouse roof;
- mathematical curves becoming a suspension bridge or orbiting mobile;
- code blocks becoming a kinetic mechanical theatre when code is actually the subject;
- a small sketched creature gaining paper volume and walking between notes;
- a landscape assembled from reference clippings and drawn additions.

No generic “digital universe”, “data tunnel”, “neural sphere”, or starscape unless the conversation literally concerns astronomy.

## 8. Camera language

- 24–50 mm equivalent for most DESK scenes; close-ups earned by emotion or detail;
- top-down is a recurring grammar for collaboration, not every other shot;
- long lens only for compressed quiet moments;
- WORLD can break scale, but the entry should preserve a spatial match from DESK;
- camera should cross paper edges, windows, folds, or foreground objects to motivate transitions.

Avoid endless centered symmetry. GPT should not constantly sit dead-center like a product mascot.

## 9. Lighting

Baseline: soft daylight, warm paper bounce, graphite neutral.

Light evolves with relationship:
- opening: cool-neutral morning blankness;
- playful middle: warmer bounce and richer cobalt/sunflower interaction;
- misread: same room, slightly harder directional contrast — no “evil red mode”;
- revision: high clarity, layered shadows from proof sheets;
- ending: calm late-afternoon warmth with both authors' colours visible.

## 10. Six style-frame requirements

Every style frame must:
- be rendered at 1920×1080;
- read at 320 px wide;
- contain at least three depth layers;
- show a specific interaction between GPT and user;
- avoid explanatory labels except text that belongs to the scene;
- include a written review noting focal point, silhouette read, colour semantics, and whether it passes the “generic AI” test.

A frame is rejected if a reviewer could plausibly describe it as “futuristic AI assistant concept art”.
