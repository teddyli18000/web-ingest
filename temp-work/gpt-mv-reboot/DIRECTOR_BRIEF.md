# Director brief — GPT MV reboot

## 0. What this film is

This is a **character music video about GPT and a user sharing a working space**. It is not a product demo, architecture explainer, benchmark reel, or logo animation.

The audience should leave remembering a character and a relationship, not tokenization, attention graphs, tools, or a list of capabilities.

The governing question for every shot is:

> **What is happening between GPT and the user in this exact moment?**

If a shot can only be justified by “it looks AI-ish”, it is rejected.

## 1. Story spine

The film is one long creative session.

The user never receives a conventional on-camera face. Their presence is expressed through a cursor, handwriting, highlighted text, dropped files, moved objects, erased marks, pauses, and the empty chair opposite GPT.

GPT is physically present in a bright editorial studio that behaves like a shared document. She does not live inside a black machine room. The room changes because of the conversation.

Narrative arc:

1. **Blank page.** A clean table, a blinking insertion point, an empty opposite chair. GPT appears only after the user begins.
2. **Learning the shape of the request.** She listens, rearranges fragments on the table, opens drafts, tries one interpretation, then another.
3. **Making worlds.** The shared desk expands into full-frame imagined places: diagrams become architecture, prose becomes weather, code becomes moving machinery, a tiny doodle becomes a landscape. The user keeps changing the brief; GPT adapts rather than “powering up”.
4. **Misread.** A confident draft is wrong. The film does not use a red ERROR screen. Instead the physical composition fails: a paper bridge misses its landing, a sentence folds into the wrong picture, annotations circle the misunderstanding. GPT notices before the user finishes correcting it.
5. **Revision.** Multiple abandoned drafts remain visible as translucent proof sheets and graphite ghosts. GPT is not ashamed or sinister; she becomes more precise. The user edits alongside her.
6. **Shared construction.** Their contributions become difficult to separate. The cursor places one object; GPT completes the surrounding scene. GPT leaves a blank; the user fills it. The film reaches its densest, most joyful visual section here.
7. **Quiet ending.** The constructed world collapses back into the desk. The final image is not “AI alone in darkness”. GPT and the user are represented by two active marks on the same page, with room left to continue.

The emotional motion is **curiosity → play → overconfidence → correction → collaboration → quiet openness**.

## 2. Three picture modes

We use three original modes. They are not the reference film's interface/plate/code system.

### A. DESK — the shared reality

A luminous editorial studio / working table. Paper, glass, masking tape, index cards, printed images, marginal notes, an unobtrusive chat/document surface, and the user's cursor all coexist physically.

DESK answers: **what are we doing together right now?**

Rules:
- warm off-white room, real shadows, tactile material;
- no generic floating hologram UI;
- UI elements behave like objects with weight and position;
- the opposite chair or the user's working edge may enter frame, but never a user face;
- GPT can touch and move objects, but never grabs the user's cursor or hand.

### B. WORLD — a thought made concrete

The desk opens into the thing being imagined. It may be a city, storm, garden, proof, machine, constellation, room, creature, or impossible editorial collage.

WORLD answers: **what did the user's idea become in GPT's imagination?**

Rules:
- concrete, readable imagery first; abstraction only after the subject is established;
- the first frame of a WORLD shot must be nameable without explanatory text;
- composition should feel illustrated/editorial rather than 3D tech demo;
- GPT may appear inside the world as maker, guide, actor, or scale reference;
- the user's presence remains as one small yellow intervention: a cursor, sticky note, cut line, or placed object.

### C. TRACE — revisions, alternatives, and residue

Graphite construction lines, proof sheets, crop marks, strike-throughs, alternate poses, erased sentences, registration offsets, and translucent discarded versions.

TRACE answers: **what changed, and what did we learn from the change?**

Rules:
- TRACE is not “system internals”. No neural-network diagrams, matrices, token clouds, terminal spam, or fake source code unless the user's actual subject in the scene is code;
- alternate drafts look like creative work-in-progress, not hallucinated parallel universes;
- correction red appears only as an editor's mark, never as a cyber alarm wash;
- TRACE must resolve into a more intentional composition, not just glitch out.

## 3. Character direction: GPT

GPT is a **young adult woman, editorial rather than futuristic**.

Core silhouette:
- short dark bob, asymmetrical but practical;
- one narrow cobalt-blue fabric/ribbon accent woven into the hair or collar;
- off-white oversized work shirt/jacket with graphite seams;
- dark straight trousers or skirt depending on final silhouette test;
- comfortable shoes; no bodysuit, armor, glowing circuitry, halo, cat ears, wings, cables, masks, or robotic joints;
- a small folded-ribbon brooch is the recurring GPT motif; it is not the ChatGPT/OpenAI logo.

Face:
- clearly visible and expressive;
- calm eyes, strong brows, understated expressions;
- warmth comes from attention and timing, not permanent smiling;
- required expression set: listening, focused, amused, uncertain, realization, apology/correction, satisfaction, quiet rest.

Physical behavior:
- when listening: still body, eyes and hands do the work;
- when exploring: she lays out several paper possibilities rather than spawning holograms;
- when confident: gestures become economical and decisive;
- when wrong: a short stop, eye movement to the failed object, then immediate practical revision;
- when collaborating: she leaves literal space for the user to act.

Forbidden visual shorthand:
- faceless black figure;
- glowing chest core;
- neon edge outline;
- floating ring/halo;
- “AI goddess” framing;
- server-room darkness;
- Matrix rain;
- atom/network sphere centered behind the head;
- circuitry skin;
- generic cyberpunk fashion.

## 4. Colour is meaning

Palette roles:

- **paper** — warm ivory; the shared working world and neutral ground;
- **graphite** — structure, body text, construction, GPT's dark clothing/hair;
- **cobalt** — GPT's authored mark; used sparingly in ribbon, annotations, selected shapes, and key words;
- **sunflower** — the user's authored mark; cursor, sticky note, selection, placed object;
- **vermilion** — correction only; proof marks, strike-through, mismatch; never a full-screen state;
- **soft green** — completion/acceptance in rare final accents only, not an “AI green” base palette.

Colour cannot change merely to make a section more exciting. A new colour enters when the relationship or status changes.

## 5. Typography

- serif: lyrical / reflective GPT voice;
- sans: user actions, labels, ordinary shared-document text;
- mono: genuine code/data only when the content itself calls for it, never as ambient “computer” texture;
- handwritten pencil/grease-pencil: TRACE annotations and the user's quick edits.

Large type is treated as a physical graphic object in the composition. No floating HUD captions over unrelated imagery.

## 6. Shot construction rules

Every shot must have at least:

1. **ground** — room, paper, sky, wall, floor, or illustrated field;
2. **subject** — character/object/action strong enough to hold the frame;
3. **near layer** — paper edge, glass reflection, foreground object, cursor, crop mark, hand, type, weather, or moving occluder.

A fourth layer is encouraged for important shots.

Every shot definition must record:
- moment: what happens between GPT and user;
- mode: DESK / WORLD / TRACE;
- dominant action verb;
- what changes inside the shot;
- what causes the cut out;
- user mark present? yes/no and where;
- GPT expression/pose;
- palette state;
- review frame times.

## 7. Motion rules

- no opacity-only entrance for primary subjects;
- large motion has anticipation and settle;
- secondary objects react after the primary move;
- camera moves only when motivated by attention or scale;
- no perpetual slow push-in as a substitute for animation;
- no random particles;
- no screen shake unless a physical event in the scene justifies it;
- no RGB split/glitch as generic intensity;
- transitions inherit an object or gesture from the previous shot whenever possible: page turn, ruled line, match cut, fold, wipe by a foreground object, light change, camera crossing a paper edge.

## 8. Density and pacing

The failed prototypes had five large explanatory scenes. The reboot must behave like an authored MV.

Target:
- average shot length roughly 1.5–3.5 s in active sections;
- longer 4–7 s holds only when the emotion benefits from stillness;
- visual motifs recur with changed meaning rather than introducing a new gimmick every section;
- a section should contain internal beats, not one composition that merely loops.

## 9. Style-frame gate

Before an animatic exists, render six independent 1920×1080 frames:

1. **Blank page / first appearance** — GPT at the shared desk, user only as a yellow insertion point and moved chair.
2. **World-making** — a concrete imagined city/landscape physically unfolding from paper across the table.
3. **Play** — GPT and the user's cursor jointly rearranging a ridiculous but beautiful construction; warm, lively, not comedic UI.
4. **Misread** — a visually clear failed interpretation and GPT realizing it; vermilion editor marks enter for the first time.
5. **Revision** — several transparent proof layers aligning into one corrected image around GPT.
6. **Final quiet frame** — both cobalt and sunflower marks remain active on one unfinished page; GPT is present, calm, and the frame invites continuation.

A frame fails if:
- it could be mistaken for a SaaS hero graphic;
- it looks cyberpunk, occult, or dystopian without story justification;
- GPT could be replaced by any generic AI mascot with no loss;
- the user's presence is missing;
- there is no tactile depth;
- the focal subject is unclear at thumbnail size.

## 10. Review contract

Work section by section.

For every new section:
1. make storyboard cards;
2. render first / key / last frames;
3. inspect them visually at full size and thumbnail size;
4. render a short motion sample;
5. revise until the section reads without explanation;
6. only then move on.

Do not call a section done because the renderer ran successfully.

For the final film:
- full decode test;
- audio/video sync measurement;
- cut-grid audit;
- flash/safety audit;
- luma audit;
- regression sheets for sections not intentionally changed;
- final-frame hold inspection;
- human visual review of the entire preview before high-resolution export.
