# LABS basin transfer and descent-operator dependence — 2026-10-29

## Current verdict

**There is measurable cross-length structure in a narrowly defined steepest-descent basin transition, but no established transferable *improvement policy*.** In particular, basin identity is sharply dependent on the local descent operator. Do **not** claim a universal physical sqrt(N) basin law or neural search breakthrough. No E<578 was found.

This replaces the overly optimistic interpretation of pilot 01. A deterministic basin is the preimage of a **specified descent map**, not an intrinsic partition of the energy function independent of the algorithm.

## Exact protocol and tools

- LABS E(s)=sum_{k=1}^{N-1} (sum_i s_i s_{i+k})^2; all energies integer.
- Start at a uniformly random sign vector; descend by (a) **steepest** strictly energy-decreasing single flip (lowest index resolves ties), or (b) **first** strictly decreasing flip in ascending index order.
- For each run, perform 40 random **four-bit** proposals, accepting only strictly improved relaxed endpoints. Probe the fixed base at stages 0, 10, 40, using k-bit uniformly random distinct-index perturbations followed by exactly the same descent routine. All N have 150 independently generated bases, eight proposals per radius per base.
- Basin equality = equality of the **canonical symmetry orbit representative** (reversal, global negation, alternation). Compare exact sequence after relaxation, not energy equality.
- k50 is **linearly interpolated** between observed k samples where the conditional probability of leaving this basin crosses 50%; it is a descriptive estimate, not a proof or a confidence interval.
- A descent "sweep" evaluates all N one-bit neighbors, using exact O(N^2) delta computation; proxy cost = number of sweeps = local descent steps + 1, **not** wall-time or exact instruction count.
- All code under this topic: [reproducing generator](../geometry_repro.py), [cross-operator agreement](../operator_agreement.py). Run with Python + numpy + numba + pandas (+scipy for operator agreement).
- Generator's selftest compares every incremental delta with independent direct energy, checks post-flip correlation vectors, checks the exact N=100 official 578 sequence, checks that it is local under both descent operators, and verifies a derived exact barrier-sum identity.

## Different descent operator: refutes universal geometry

Replace steepest decreasing neighbor by first decreasing neighbor (ascending index order), holding other parameters and N=100 stage 40 the same (new seed 592104). ## Exact mathematical constraint on mean single-flip barriers

For sequence s, C_k = sum_i s_i s_{i+k}, and Δ_i E = E(s with bit i flipped) - E(s). Summing over all sites i,

\[
\boxed{\sum_{i=1}^{N}\Delta_i E = 4N(N-1) - 8 E(s) + 8\sum_{k=1}^{\lfloor (N-1)/2\rfloor} C_{2k}(s).}
\]

Proof: under flip i, Δ_i C_k = -2 s_i(s_{i-k} 1_{i>=k} + s_{i+k} 1_{i+k<N}); hence sum_i Δ_i C_k = -4 C_k, and sum_i(Δ_i C_k)^2 = 8(N-k)+8 C_{2k}, interpreting C_{2k}=0 when 2k>=N. Expand (C_k+Δ_i C_k)^2-C_k^2 and sum over k,i. This is an **exact identity**, not a novelty claim (no claim of priority). Independently checked on randomized integer sequences N12,24,40,64,100. At the N100 E578 certificate, the average one-bit barrier equals **349.76**, checking local stiffness directly. This gives a principled feature for learning conditional basin response, while warning that energy and mean barrier are algebraically coupled.

## Predictor test (secondary negative evidence, not algorithm victory)

A local experiment generated 120 independent bases at each of N24,28,32,40,44,64,100 (8 candidates each k=2,4,6,8; 3 trajectory stages), and trained histogram gradient boosting on N24/28/32, tested N40/44/64/100, with fixed-k candidate pools and **positive improvement of energy** as the selection objective. Cheap pre-relax features included immediate energy, single-spin barrier statistics and size; expanded variants added pair interactions and autocorrelation-spectrum summaries. All labels were computed by exact independent descent; model predictions used no post-relax information.

At N100 stage40, 120 pools per k, *one relaxation per selected 8-candidate pool*: for k6, simple sum-of-single-flip-deltas achieved 10 improvement hits; all-stage cheap regression 12 hits; elite-trained cheap improvement classifier 10 hits; elite-trained structural classifier 3 hits; full eight-candidate oracle 36 hits. For k8, simple sum-deltas 8, elite structural classifier 8, cheap regression 8, oracle 35. Bootstrap 95% confidence intervals on **paired per-pool positive-gain differences** versus sum-deltas included zero for the tested learned methods. This supports **no robust transferred improvement**; a lower predicted final energy can merely mean returning to the same basin more frequently. Model inference overhead and stronger solver baselines were not yet charged, so cannot claim fair search speedups. The current generator and analysis for this secondary pilot still need consolidation into a single tracked reproduction harness; treat it as exploratory evidence.

## Research decision and next tests

1. **Do not scale a neural network on exact-basin labels.** Basin identity/action ranking depends strongly on descent map.
2. Design a **descent-ensemble value target**: for each perturbation, evaluate a small, fixed mixture of steepest and first-descent endpoints, or randomized tie-breaking; ask whether a cheap, symmetry-invariant, size-normalized feature predicts *positive gain per compute* across operators and N. Test both train/test lengths and entirely held-out descent rules.
3. Factor search into **radius scheduling** (candidate scale, basin stiffness and energy level) and **within-radius candidate choice**. Compare to k ≈ a sqrt(N), k fixed, and adaptive bandit using *matched wall time/neighbor scans*. The high-energy stage40 vs official E578 gap must be explicit.
4. Try an alternate **finite-temperature committor-like** quantity (probability that a randomized descent beats current energy within fixed budget) rather than a deterministic basin ID. Strong Bayesian/bandit and classical heuristic baselines are mandatory.
5. Keep all claims separate: structure observations vs transferable policy vs genuine N100 improvement. The LABS100 official submission owner remains science-boundary/labs-100; this study never modifies that topic.
