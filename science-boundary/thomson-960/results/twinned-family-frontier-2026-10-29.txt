Thomson N=960 — twinned grain-boundary family frontier
Date: 2026-10-29

Motivation
----------
The current low-energy N=960 graph R/A/B/D and the C index-2 stationary state
all belong to the same 24-dislocation scar composition: 12 alternating
5-7-5-7-5 scars, with no intended 7-7 twin core.  Extensive local, Hessian,
size-bounce, crossover and transition-network exploration has mainly moved
within this topology family.

A distinct defect family is documented in the historical Thomson literature:

- Wales & Ulker, Phys. Rev. B 74, 212101 (2006), DOI 10.1103/PhysRevB.74.212101.
  Selected large-N structures include a 'twinned' defect in which two
  seven-coordinated sites share a Voronoi/Delaunay edge and are surrounded by
  three pentagons, net topological charge +1.  N=912, 932 and 972 are explicit
  examples in the paper.
- Wales, McKay & Altschuler, Phys. Rev. B 79, 224115 (2009),
  DOI 10.1103/PhysRevB.79.224115.  Around N~1000, twinned grain boundaries are
  reported to become favorable before rosette motifs dominate at somewhat
  larger N.
- Historical coordinates are available from the Cambridge Cluster Database,
  including 912.xyz, 932.xyz and 972.xyz under CCD/Thomson/xyz.  The historical
  table identifies the N=972 structure as T_h symmetric.

A particularly strong arithmetic/topological match makes N=972 important rather
than merely nearby in particle number.  Table I of Wales & Ulker gives the N=972
historical minimum coordination as 36 pentagons / 912 hexagons / 24 heptagons.
The current N=960 record family is 36 / 900 / 24.  Therefore the 12-particle
size difference can, in principle, be absorbed entirely by deleting twelve
sixfold bulk sites while leaving the complete defect count unchanged.  The
historical N=972 minimum nevertheless contains the distinct twinned/grain-boundary
arrangement.  This makes 972->960 a direct bridge between two defect organizations
with identical 5/7 counts at the target.

The same 2006 paper also reports an N=972 transition between twinned and
conventional grain-boundary minima with forward/reverse barriers about 0.00424
and 0.00104 atomic units.  Thus these defect organizations are already known to
be near neighbors on the N=972 energy landscape, strengthening the case for
searching the corresponding topology interface at N=960 rather than assuming
one family dominates by a large barrier.

This creates a genuinely different search space from further perturbation of
R/A/B/D.

Topology discriminator
----------------------
`code/analyze_twinned_motif.py` computes spherical Delaunay coordination and
counts 7-7 edges.  A direct heptagon-heptagon edge is the core signature of the
historical twinned motif and distinguishes it from an ordinary alternating
5-7-5-7-5 scar.

Diagnostic workflow: `.github/workflows/temp-thomson-960-twinned-diagnostic.yml`
run 40798144771, matrix N=912/932/942/972.  It was queued when this note was
written.

Search operator 1: topology-preserving size propagation
-------------------------------------------------------
`code/propagate_twinned_family.py` propagates a historical parent to N=960 while
actively retaining the topology family:

- defect core = all non-sixfold sites, expanded by configurable Delaunay graph
  distance (default 2 hops);
- insertions are allowed only on convex-hull triangles outside that core;
- removals are allowed only for sixfold sites outside the core;
- beam selection first favors descendants retaining at least one 7-7 edge,
  then ranks by energy;
- all children receive full unconstrained stable long-double-difference L-BFGS
  relaxation.

This is not the same as the merged-shot dead end that greedily removed 12
particles from N=972: energy-only greedy propagation can immediately heal away
the twin family.  This operator treats persistence of the defect topology as a
search coordinate.

The 972->960 route was launched via run 40798582516, using branch=2, beam=2,
core_hops=2 by default.  It was queued when this note was written.

Search operator 2: one complete tetrahedral orbit removal
----------------------------------------------------------
N=972 -> N=960 is exactly 12 particles.  The proper tetrahedral rotation group
T has generic orbit size 12, while the historical N=972 parent is T_h symmetric.
This arithmetic/symmetry coincidence motivates a finite discrete search that is
not represented by sequential deletion.  It is even more natural given the
coordination counts above: a 12-point orbit consisting entirely of bulk
sixfold sites maps 36/912/24 exactly to 36/900/24 before any topology change.

1. recover one exact C2 and one C3 symmetry axis from the N=972 point cloud by
   nearest-neighbor symmetry fitting;
2. generate/validate the proper T group of order 12;
3. enumerate point orbits under T;
4. retain only full 12-point orbits composed entirely of sixfold bulk sites at
   least two graph hops from the defect core;
5. remove one entire orbit, giving N=960 in a single symmetry-preserving move;
6. screen by raw post-removal energy, then fully release the best children;
7. record whether 7-7 twin edges survive.

Implementation: `code/search_972_twin_orbit_removal.py`.
Workflow: `.github/workflows/temp-thomson-960-972-orbit-removal.yml`.
This route was submitted on main and awaiting Actions scheduling when this note
was written.

Decision rule
-------------
- If a twinned-family N=960 child is below live_score-2e-7, immediately stop
  exploration and run the stable repolish -> rationalization -> official
  verifier -> tools/check.py pipeline.
- If the family survives to N=960 but remains above the record, preserve the
  lowest child as a distinct topology seed and explore its own local/symmetry
  landscape rather than forcing it back into the R/A/B/D scar network.
- If every constrained/topology-preserving route loses all 7-7 edges before
  reaching N=960, that is meaningful evidence that this historical twin family
  is not locally stable at N=960 under these propagation interfaces; it is not
  a proof that no twinned N=960 minimum exists.
