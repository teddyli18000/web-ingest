Motivation from latest literature
---------------------------------
Amore & Wales, J. Chem. Phys. 165, 164104 (2026), arXiv:2610.08122, introduce an Oscillating Size Hybrid Basin-Hopping (OSHBH) strategy.  At N->N+1 they add a charge at spherical Voronoi vertices, rank candidates by the individual Coulomb energy of the inserted charge, relax a small number of the best candidates, and propagate the best structures across neighbouring sizes.  At N->N-1 they rank point removals by the removed charge's individual energy contribution.  The search can bounce at the size limits.

Their published N=960 value is 444368.43734798, well above the current Few Shots record, so their N=960 structure is not a competitive parent.  The scientifically interesting experiment is instead to start OSHBH-style size changes from the much stronger live record and use temporary changes in N as a barrier-crossing representation.

Interpretation
--------------
The greedy neighbouring-size move is therefore not spatially random around the current record:
- N->N+1 preferentially inserts near 7-fold defect environments;
- N->N-1 preferentially removes 5-fold defect vertices.

A temporary size change can thus screen/reconstruct one end of a scar and alter its elastic/topological compatibility before returning to N=960.  This is qualitatively different from the fixed-N Delaunay glide/scaffold searches, where local scar changes repeatedly heal unless a sufficiently long history-dependent and remote-follower cascade is organised.
