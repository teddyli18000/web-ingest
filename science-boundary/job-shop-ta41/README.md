## Distance is not coordination — exact experimental distinction (2026-10-29)

**See [distance-vs-coordination-2026-10-29.md](distance-vs-coordination-2026-10-29.md) for full evidence, exact mathematical definitions, and result provenance.** The earlier claim that >=15 changed machine orders implies a very distant 2005 solution is **unjustified**: a Few Shots-officially validated makespan-2006 schedule has exactly **15 Kendall machine-pair inversions spread across 15 machines**, obtained by solving all swaps atomically (`results/synchronous-single-2006/`). A second valid 2006 schedule has 18 inversions on 15 machines. These are genuine extremely small perturbations in the disjunctive pair metric, not wholesale order changes.

**Exact coordination experiment:** enumerate every one of the 2^15 = **32768** subsets of the fifteen verified adjacent swaps, evaluating the full DAG; minimize the peak makespan over *every monotone ordering* using exact subset DP. The minimum peak is **2006**, so there exists a flat 2006 path. But machine 1 position 8 swap **alone scores 2024** and requires **two enabling neutral swaps** (machines 4 pos10 and 2 pos10) before it can be executed without uphill; machine 17 position17 swap **alone scores 2047** and requires **one enabling swap** (machine 5 pos16). These minimal prerequisites are **exact** within the 15-swap lattice (`results/exact-15-swap-coordination-lattice.json`). This is a concrete, complete example of search interaction: naive individual-step scoring can reject changes which belong to a zero-barrier coordinated path.

**Limit:** These 15 swaps reverse **none** of the 21 mandatory precedence relations in the previously certified no-2005 core. Therefore this cohort cannot reach 2005. The exact Kendall-radius 2005 decision is **UNKNOWN for unrestricted radii 20 and 40 after 210s each**. Restrictions only permitting rank-gap<=2 reversals at radius20, or rank-gap<=3 at radius30, are INFEASIBLE, but do not exclude unrestricted nearby solutions. No proof yet whether the nearest <=2005 schedule is near or distant. Currently launched `gateway_kendall_cost.py` optimizes the minimal Kendall change needed to reverse one of three **necessary core gateway arcs** while remaining at <=2006; it is **pending**, not a proven bound.

**Next:** search for coordinated, mutually enabling *macro-moves* that cross a necessary 21-core precedence gateway, not merely for a large random perturbation. Distinguish invariant/neutral 2006 cores from candidates with actual 2005 potential. Judge every candidate by official Few Shots verification and never promote UNKNOWN to infeasibility.

## 2026-10-29: dynamic critical-path replacement repair experiment

- **Hypothesis:** letting every accepted micro-move's newly created critical machine arcs select the next N7-inspired insertion and operation-level joint CP release can outperform static critical-path, fixed-core, and random attention at matched exact-score budgets. This is an original DCPRR mechanism probe inspired by Ba–Liu–Yuan's 2026 N7 *batch evaluation* and Liu–Li–Gao's 2026 *decision-variable release* record (ScheduleOpt); it does **not** implement either full paper.
- **Controlled pilot 8/8 completed:** two independently verified Cmax=2006 parents (original, gateway-124); four selectors (dynamic/static/core/random), each 18,000 exact DAG candidate evaluations at common seed 4107. **All eight remain at 2006, zero <=2005**. Dynamic policy produces many new critical transitions (baseline 296 events, gateway-124 662), showing real feedback but **no score advantage**. See `results/dcpr-{baseline,124}-{dynamic,static,core,random}-4107/pilot.json`.
- **Growing CP operation release, 64/64 complete:** eight rounds/selector/parent, +30 dynamically selected free operations per round (max 240), all remaining survivor precedence arcs fixed, full job and machine NoOverlap. **All 64 exact decision subspaces INFEASIBLE** for Cmax<=2005. Rebuilding final eight models with survivor arcs as assumptions extracted sufficient UNSAT cores of **59–95 still-frozen machine precedences spanning 9–17 machines**. Thus newly critical operations alone do not identify every coupled resource conflict. Sources: `results/dcprcp-*/dynamic-repair.json`; `results/dcprcp-independent-core-audit.json`.
- **Core-feedback follow-up:** starting from each parent's dynamic 240-release set, use CP assumption cores to select further 24-operation releases instead of simply continuing N7. Three controls: core-degree, core-random, unrelated random. The first core oracle UNKNOWN arose at 288–336 free operations; importantly an UNKNOWN is not evidence of a feasible target. Six full feasible-hinted CP objective models were then rerun: **five OPTIMAL=2006**; sixth (gateway-124 random, 336 free) **FEASIBLE=2006 with lower bound 1996** at 105s, unresolved. No certificate <=2005. Sources `results/core-feedback-*/core-release.json`, `results/core-objective-*/objective-repair.json`.
- **Conclusion:** In this registered operator/decoder regime, dynamic critical-path replacement is mechanically active but **has not improved the best verified score**. The limiting interface is now plausibly hundreds of still-hard survivor precedences. **Do not just scale this fixed-survivor release family**. The next qualitatively distinct model should jointly reorient soft/order variables using old + successor bottlenecks and learned resource conflict cores, without preserving all nonreleased survivor pairs as hard constraints.

## First-principles reformulation (2026-10-28)
Rather than only vary local-swap parameters, ask the **binary feasibility** question: Does any schedule exist with makespan <= 2005? The incumbent's 580 adjacent machine precedences are individually optional clauses. We impose 600 true intervals, 20 global `NoOverlap` constraints, and job precedence unconditionally. The incumbent machine arcs become reified assumptions (keep or relax). Every UNSAT core produced under a subset of those assumptions is a **sufficient obstruction**: any better schedule must violate at least one retained machine-arc assumption in the core. The problem of minimally relaxing the incumbent therefore becomes a **hitting-set problem over learned cores**, followed by an exact CP-SAT feasibility check with the remaining 580 - |relaxed| arcs pinned. This is a falsifiable approach, not a claim of a novel general algorithm or a proof of infeasibility.

`core_guided_lns.py` implements that mechanism with deterministic seed, exact processing times, independent DAG rescore, and persisted evidence. An UNSAT response with assumptions only proves infeasibility **within that fixed-arc subspace**; UNKNOWN proves nothing. When a candidate is found, the Few Shots verifier is invoked on the result before it is trusted. See the one-shot `temp-ta41-core-guided.yml` action. This program requires OR-Tools CP-SAT 9.14.6206 (installed and pinned by the Action).

The hypothesis being tested is **coupled structural rigidity**: changing only critical-path-machine orders is now rigorously ruled out by 299 CP-SAT subspace exclusions, while exact radius-2 adjacent-swap enumeration is also negative. If learned cores remain dense and the derived hitting set grows without producing improvements, change decomposition (partition jobs/machines by cut structure, or search start-time freedom directly), rather than extending the same search blindly.

### Key interpretation
The single-edge critical cut is a **lower-bound relaxation**; the machine permutation creates replacement edges and nonlocal delays. The structural research question is therefore **coupled replacement of resource-order constraints**, not merely selection of an old bottleneck edge to delete. The exact machine-subset exclusions demonstrate a hidden dependency on machines that carry no longest-path machine arcs in the incumbent. A solution route worth testing next is to let a solver identify a small set of **whole machines** (group assumptions), rather than individual adjacent arcs, that jointly must be released, and to solve coupled time windows over those machines.

## Search limitations / next research
Critical-path N1 adjacent moves can stall in local optima; a lack of improvement says nothing about global optimality. Next: richer N5/N6 block moves, path-relinking and elite pool, time-window CP-SAT/LNS, and stronger backbone/cut analysis. Avoid repeating identical seeds/tenures without changing the representation.
