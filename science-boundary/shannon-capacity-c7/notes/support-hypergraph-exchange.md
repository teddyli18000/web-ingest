# Support-hypergraph formulation for unrestricted C7^10 exchanges

For a fixed independent set `I` in `C7^10`, define the blocker support of any outside vertex `v` by

`S(v) = N[v] ∩ I`.

A net `+2` exchange of radius `r` exists exactly when there are `r+2` pairwise nonconfusable outside vertices whose support union has size at most `r`. Any unused deletion budget can be padded with arbitrary old vertices. Thus the local improvement problem is naturally a constrained independent-set problem over candidate vertices labelled by small support hyperedges, rather than a geometric Hamming-ball search around the original BPZ construction.

For the FunSearch t=9 one-hit deletion seeds, full-space transforms give `coverage0=0`, so all useful candidates have nonempty support. Exact unrestricted searches have already ruled out net `+2` through support-union size 4, and the exact radius-5 closure is being completed over the four C5 orbit representatives.

## Why support connectivity matters

For an owner set `U`, write `P(U)` for every outside vertex with support contained in `U`. A negative radius-`s` result implies `alpha(P(U)) <= s+1` for every `|U| <= s`: otherwise an independent `s+2`-set in `P(U)` would be a net `+2` exchange after padding deletions.

Additional structure is stronger here:

- the exact radius-3 replay records no compatible pair inside any one-owner coverage-one group on the checked representatives (`heavy_c1_owners=0` where reported);
- the radius-4 precondition/census gives independence number at most 2 for every genuine two-owner pool on the seeds to which the exact radius-4 solver applies;
- exact radius-3 negativity gives every three-owner pool independence number at most 4;
- exact radius-4 negativity gives every four-owner pool independence number at most 5.

These inequalities sharply restrict disconnected support patterns. At radius 4, if six selected additions use four owners and their support hypergraph is disconnected, the owner partition is either `3+1` or `2+2`. The former contributes at most `4+1=5`; the latter at most `2+2=4`. Therefore any radius-4 witness without a coverage-4 candidate must have a connected support graph across all four owners.

This yields a complete owner-quadruple generator:

1. direct support of a coverage-4 candidate;
2. union of two coverage-3 supports that expose four owners;
3. a coverage-3 support plus a crossing coverage-2 support;
4. with only coverage-one/two candidates, a connected coverage-two support graph, generated by a two-edge wedge plus an extending edge (a spanning tree on four owners).

For each generated owner quadruple, replay the complete candidate pool from all support groups contained in it and solve the tiny induced independent-set problem exactly for six additions.

## Radius 5

Once radius 4 is negative, `alpha(P(U)) <= 5` for every four-owner set. Together with the lower-radius component bounds, every disconnected partition of five owners supports at most six pairwise nonconfusable additions. Therefore a seven-addition radius-5 witness must have a connected support hypergraph across all five owners unless it contains a direct coverage-5 support, which is handled separately.

The exact radius-5 search therefore has two complementary stages:

1. direct owner-five supports coming from coverage-5 candidates;
2. connected lower-support owner-five sets generated from coverage-2/3/4 support expansions.

Both stages always reconstruct the complete pool `P(U)` before solving the induced independent-set problem; the support generator only reduces which owner sets need to be considered.

## Radius 6 Hall-type constraints

Assume the exact radius-5 search is negative, so for every owner set `W` with `|W| <= 5`, the certified lower-radius bounds apply. Consider a putative radius-6 witness: eight pairwise nonconfusable additions whose total owner union is a six-set `U`.

For any subset `T ⊆ U`, additions whose supports avoid `T` all lie in `P(U \ T)`. Hence

`# additions whose support intersects T >= 8 - alpha(P(U \ T))`.

Using the exact lower-radius bounds gives a Hall-type expansion requirement. In particular, for `|T|=t` and the generic bound `alpha(P(U\T)) <= |U\T|+1`, at least `t+1` selected additions must meet `T`. Important special cases are:

- every individual owner must occur in the support of at least two selected additions;
- every pair of owners must be met by at least three selected additions;
- every three-owner subset must be met by at least four selected additions.

The stronger singleton/two-owner replay bounds sharpen the last cases further when they have been independently checked for the seed being searched. Any radius-6 generator should enforce these inequalities before solving the candidate pool. In particular, connected owner-six sets with a leaf owner of support-degree one can be discarded immediately.

### Disconnected radius-6 exception

With the stronger one-owner and two-owner bounds replayed at runtime, all disconnected partitions of six owners fall below eight additions except `3+3`. The only possible disconnected radius-6 witness is therefore two disjoint three-owner components, each attaining `alpha(P(U_i)) = 4`, with the two selected independent 4-sets also mutually cross-compatible.

Moreover a three-owner pool attaining four additions must itself use connected support: if its selected support split over a `2+1` owner partition, the exact two-owner and one-owner bounds would give at most `2+1=3`. Thus productive three-owner pools for the `3+3` exception are completely generated by either a direct coverage-3 support or at least two distinct crossing coverage-2 supports that expose all three owners.

This motivates an exact two-stage radius-6 decomposition:

1. census and pair all saturated three-owner components for the disconnected `3+3` case;
2. handle genuinely connected six-owner support sets with the Hall-type incidence constraints above.

## Exact support recovery without high-order moments

For radius 6 and beyond, high-order owner moments become awkward. A cleaner exact representation is an in-place support-list transform. Store, at every one of the `7^10` ambient vertices, the sorted list of blocker-owner labels while its size is at most six, otherwise an overflow marker. Expanding one coordinate replaces a record by the union of the three cyclic neighboring records.

Support size is monotone during the ten coordinate expansions. Therefore any final ambient vertex with blocker coverage at most six never overflowed earlier, and its complete owner support is recovered exactly. The transform can be performed in place because each disjoint seven-cycle group is copied to stack storage before its seven new records are written back. With six `uint32` labels per ambient vertex, the main array uses about 6.8 GB decimal (about 6.3 GiB), avoiding a second full-size transform buffer and avoiding quartic/moment inversion entirely.

Reusable pilot: `code/census_support6_lists.cpp`.

## Completeness discipline

The invariant to preserve is completeness: computational failure only excludes the owner/support families actually generated. Every reduction from all owner sets to a smaller support family needs an explicit combinatorial argument using already-certified lower-radius independence bounds. Stronger empirical properties such as singleton-clique or two-owner `alpha <= 2` must be replayed or checked for every symmetry representative on which they are used; they must not be silently extrapolated from case 0.
